# 04 — Clinic Landscape

Purpose: create a transparent selection basis for discussion with RFSU without pre-selecting the three clinics.

## Canonical selection funnel

**ALL → GATE → RELEVANCE → PALETTE → RFSU DIALOGUE → THREE CASES**

## 01 National longlist — ALL

Create an orienting national universe of potentially relevant adult sexual-health services.

This stage is inclusive. It is not a quality ranking.

## 02 Geography

Map where potentially relevant services are located and identify regional/systemic patterns when geography materially affects the analysis.

Geographic spread is not an end in itself.

## 03 Gate

A clinic proceeds only when there is adequate support for the assignment's distinct requirements:

- adult/post-youth-clinic function;
- sexual health as a core task;
- holistic practice;
- multidisciplinary practice;
- person-centred practice.

Use:
- PASS
- UNCLEAR
- FAIL

**UNCLEAR must not be silently upgraded to PASS.**

Gate determines fit to the study object, not the quality of the service.

## 04 Relevance profiles

For Gate-passing clinics, describe what each could contribute to the assignment's learning.

Dimensions may include:
- organisational form;
- governance/commissioning model;
- position in the care pathway;
- SRHR breadth;
- professional configuration;
- access logic;
- equity profile;
- structural traceability;
- transferability potential;
- practical feasibility.

Do not calculate a total score or rank clinics from best to worst.

## 05 Palette

Build a discussion palette showing analytically different case possibilities, for example:
- broad adult SRHR model;
- regional SESAM-type model;
- target-group adapted equity model;
- multidisciplinary specialist model;
- private/third-sector delivery on public commission;
- hybrid physical/digital access model.

The palette is not a shortlist.

## 06 Selection dialogue

Final selection belongs to the joint RFSU–consultant process.

Consultant contributes:
- landscape;
- Gate verification;
- relevance profiles;
- analytical palette;
- consequences of alternative case combinations.

RFSU contributes:
- field knowledge;
- strategic priorities;
- existing relationships;
- practical knowledge of candidate services.

The final three cases are therefore a project decision, not a pre-award consultant claim.
